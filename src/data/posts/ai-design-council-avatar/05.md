Scores rose every round through round 4, then flattened: after round 4, only one critic's score moved, by half a point, because round 6's new jaw hair cost about what the rest gained. My feedback came after round 3, and round 4 is also when the designer became three lanes, so I can't credit any one change.

Six rounds to reach a 7? The critics scored against a "wow" bar on purpose. What matters is around 4 to around 7 in one evening.

## Making the loop fast

Round 4 was also when the workbench was rebuilt. Rendering had become the bottleneck: the full sheet took 191.6 s, one still after another, so every "did that help?" cost more than three minutes. The new workbench renders the stills in parallel on a warm pool of headless browsers. Animation runs on a virtual clock that steps exactly 1/60 s per frame, with a fixed random seed per still, so a before/after diff shows only real changes.

| Task | Time |
|---|---|
| Full sheet, before | 191.6 s |
| Full sheet, after | 18–21 s |
| One preset (sides, face, jaw, hero, overlay) | 0.8–4.1 s |
| Quick preview | 7.7 s |
| Before/after compare | 0.5 s |

## I saw a beard

After round 3, two things bothered me. The first was a gray band under her mouth and cool shading at the jaw corners. One critic had noted a "muzzle band" among many other notes. To me it was the first thing I saw: "make sure the lower part of the head doesn't look like a shading beard." The second was the pace: "this attempt looked incrementally better". That wasn't praise. I wanted bigger leaps.
