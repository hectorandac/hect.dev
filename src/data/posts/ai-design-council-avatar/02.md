## A glowing blob of cubes

Most assistants get a glowing circle. I wanted a character, and the prototype was real three.js code in the app, not a mockup.

Laura is my private, bilingual voice assistant for my phone and watch. The code is public at [github.com/hectorandac/laura-voice](https://github.com/hectorandac/laura-voice).

I knew it wasn't good, and I didn't know how to make it good. I work in computer vision and edge AI: I can tell you why a detector misses small objects, not why a face looks cheap. What I could build was a process: roles, a brief, renders and checks.

## What I did, and what the agents did

"AI made my avatar" isn't true, and "I designed it" isn't either.

I designed the process: the roles, independent proposals, one brief, fixed rounds, and the rule that only renders from the real app count. The concept was mine. After round 3, my note reset the priority to the jaw and asked for bigger leaps. The ship call was mine.

The agents wrote the proposals, critiques, fix lists and all of the three.js code, and built the checks and the render workbench.

I didn't draw a single cube.

## A council of critics and designers

Each role was an AI agent with one lens:

- A **style critic**, acting as art director: shape, value, color.
- A **UX critic** for voice assistants: does she read at 120 px over another app, are her states distinct in grayscale.
- A **concept critic**, acting as creative director: who Laura is, and whether she stays one character on the phone, the overlay and the watch.
- A **designer** who wrote the code. From round 4, that became three lanes (face, hair and silhouette, light and material), merged each round.

The critics judged only rendered stills from the real renderer, against the brief, and each wrote its own score and findings before the concept critic merged them into one fix list.

Each critic first proposed independently, and they landed close: opaque cubes, light from inside, one asymmetric signature. That became one brief, "Lantern": a porcelain voxel girl with a warm inner light, a plum hair cap and a ponytail.

The brief settled disagreements with a reason. Style wanted glowing eyes; the other two wanted dark, glossy ones. The brief picked dark eyes: glowing eyes read as a robot visor, dark eyes on pale skin keep at least 4.5:1 contrast, and a workbench check failed any eye or mouth contrast under 4.5:1.

## The loop: build, render, critique, fix

Every round, the designer changed the real three.js avatar; the workbench rendered the same fixed sheet (every state in color and in grayscale, black silhouettes, her real bubble size over a busy phone screen), growing from 16 images in round 1 to 34 plus jaw close-ups by round 6; each critic scored it out of 10; and the concept critic wrote the next fix list.
